⚡ Power Setup — LM2596 Buck Converter
DO THIS FIRST ▼
FromToWire
18650 Holder B+LM2596 IN+🔴 Red
18650 Holder B-LM2596 IN-⚫ Black
LM2596 OUT+Breadboard (+) Rail🔴 Red
LM2596 OUT-Breadboard (−) Rail⚫ Black
⚠️ CRITICAL: Use a multimeter on OUT+ and OUT−. Adjust the brass screw on LM2596 until you read exactly 5.0V. Default may be 12V which will instantly destroy your ESP32!
💡 Battery voltage (~7.4V from 2x 18650) goes into IN. Regulated 5V comes out of OUT. Always power off batteries before rewiring.

🧠 ESP32 DevKit V1 — Power Connection
Core Brain ▼

💡 Place ESP32 straddling the center gap of the breadboard. The USB port should face outward for easy programming access. ESP32 outputs 3.3V on its 3V3 pin — use this for sensors, NOT the 5V rail directly.

👁️ VL53L0X ToF Laser Sensor (GY-530)
I2C — Addr: 0x29 ▼

💡 Mount this sensor at the very front of the chassis facing forward. It gives distance readings from 2cm to 200cm with ±1mm accuracy. Install library: Adafruit VL53L0X in Arduino IDE.

🌀 MPU6050 Gyro/Accelerometer (R233573)

💡 Both VL53L0X and MPU6050 share GPIO 21 (SDA) and GPIO 22 (SCL) — this is fine! I2C supports multiple devices on the same bus. Mount MPU6050 at the center of chassis for accurate turn calculations. Install library: Adafruit MPU6050.

📡 LM393 Speed Encoder Sensors (x2)
Wheel Odometry ▼

VCC3V3Both sensors

💡 GPIO 34 and 35 are INPUT ONLY on ESP32 — perfect for encoders. Mount the IR slot sensor so the encoder disc wheel passes through the gap. Each slot = 1 pulse = known distance moved. Used for dead-reckoning navigation.

✅ Checklist before powering on:
□ Buck converter set to 5.0V verified with multimeter
□ VM on TB6612 goes to raw battery, not 5V rail
□ All sensor VCC connected to ESP32 3V3 (not 5V)
□ STBY pin on TB6612 tied to 3V3 (not floating)
□ No exposed wire bridges on breadboard
□ USB cable connected to laptop before turning on batteries
